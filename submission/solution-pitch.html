Listen twice.
Write once. Or refuse.

A wrong pour entry is money paid for milk that was never poured. PaalKural turns a spoken Tamil or Tanglish pour into one confirmed ledger entry for a milk society: two independent listens check the member, litres, fat and shift, and a disagreement blocks the write until the operator decides.

The problem

A transcript is not an entry.

●The line is loud. Fans, cans, people — the operator is writing down money by ear.

●Speech-to-text returns words, not numbers that have been checked against a roster and a range.

●A confident wrong number becomes a wrong payment, and nobody notices until the books are reconciled.

The solution · how one pour moves
01

Listen twice

The same clip is decoded raw, then again after a band-limit and spectral gate. In this build both listens are live Gnani Prisma.

02

Compile four facts

A deterministic compiler turns each transcript into member · litres · fat · shift, then checks the roster and valid ranges.

03

Gate the write

Both agree → pre-fill. One validates → pre-fill. Both valid but different → CHECK blocks the log. Neither → re-ask in Tamil.

04

Read back, confirm

The operator hears the entry in Tamil and confirms. The receipt stores transcript, correction, provider and audit hash; the day book exports to CSV.

Why it wins
✳

It refuses to guess.

A split listen stops the write. A bad turn costs a re-ask, not a wrong payout.

✳

It ships the evidence — including the bad news.

On our own 24-clip benchmark the filter alone did not beat raw. We published that negative result and built the gate that does.

✳

It is a complete product, not a filter demo.

Operator console, ledger, receipts, CSV day book, Evidence Lab, live replay and 26 automated tests.

✳

It speaks the line's language.

Tamil/Tanglish code-mix in, Tamil readback out, typed fallback when the line is too loud — all in a browser.
